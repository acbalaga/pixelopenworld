# 05 Life systems

Everything here is a complete way to play. A player who never fights must still have 100+ hours of game.

## Gathering

- Nodes (ore veins, trees, herb patches, fishing spots, forage bushes) have a tier and respawn on a real-time timer (minutes for common, an in-game day for rare).
- Gather with the context button. Each skill has a light timing input: hit the swing at the sweet spot for bonus yield and quality. Missing still yields. It is never a fail state.
- Tools (pick, axe, sickle, rod) have tiers and are crafted. Tool tier below node tier means slow and low yield, not impossible.
- Rare variants appear by weather, season, and time of day.

## Crafting

Seven craft skills, each with a station. Stations exist in hubs and can be built at home.

- **Recipes** are learned by: buying, being taught, finding, or **experimenting** (combine materials without a recipe, discover results). Experimenting is the main path and is logged in the Chronicle.
- **Quality.** Every crafted item rolls Common, Fine, Superior, or Masterwork. Quality depends on skill rank against recipe tier, material quality, and a short manual step (hammer timing, stir rhythm, stitch tracing). Automation can never roll Masterwork.
- **Maker's mark.** Items the player crafts carry their name. Travelers who buy them mention it.
- **Gear** has a base (from recipe and tier), quality, and up to two affixes added by Enchanting. No random loot affix soup.
- About 900 items total. Roughly 320 gear, 200 food and drink, 120 materials, 80 potions, 100 furniture and decor, 80 tools, keys, and oddities.

### Cooking

The most social craft. Meals give timed Fed buffs (one active at a time), restore pools, and are the best gifts. 200 recipes. Dishes can be served at camp to the whole party, sold from a stall, or entered in festival contests.

### Fishing

A calm tension minigame (keep the marker in the band, one thumb). 96 species across regions, weather, season, and time. Legendary fish per region.

## Farming and husbandry

- Plots on leased land (the reeve's field outside Hearthford, from early play), in a house's kitchen garden, and on the homestead. Till, plant, water, wait, harvest. The crop, animal, fish, tameable and furniture lists are in 13-life-content.
- Crops grow in real time including offline (the clock keeps running). Growth takes 1 to 6 in-game days by crop. Season and weather matter. Rain waters for you.
- Soil quality, crop rotation, and fertilizer give depth for those who want it. Ignoring them still grows food.
- Animals (Husbandry) need feed and attention, produce daily goods, and bond with the player. Neglect lowers output. Animals never die of neglect.

## Taming, pets, mounts

- Taming is feeding and soothing a creature with the right food and timing, sometimes after weakening it, sometimes only if you never hurt its kind that day.
- 48 tameable species. Each has a companion role (fight, carry, gather, ride, sniff out secrets).
- One active companion (two with the Beast Warden Path). Others stay at the homestead and can be assigned to Routines.

## Housing

1. **Room at the inn** (free, Hearthford). A bed and a chest.
2. **House plot** in a hub. Requires Renown and coin. Fixed footprint, free interior placement on a tile grid.
3. **Homestead.** A larger plot outside town in each region after the regional boss is resolved. Farm plots, pens, workshops, storage, a Waystone.
4. **Outposts** in the Unwritten (M11).

- Furniture is crafted or bought. 100+ pieces. Some are functional (stations, storage, trophy stands, a bookshelf that holds found lore).
- Sleeping in your own bed gives Rested (bonus skill XP for a while) and is the best logout point.
- Travelers who are friends visit. Their comments reflect what you built.

## Economy

One currency: coin.

- **NPC shops** buy and sell at stable prices with small regional differences.
- **The Market** (hub in Port Brine, terminals in every hub) is the simulated auction house. Prices come from a supply and demand model driven by Traveler activity, season, world events, and what the player sells. Flooding the market drops the price. Cornering a material is possible and Travelers will complain in chat.
- **Sinks:** housing, plots, shop licenses, repairs, Waystone crafting, festival entries, guild dues, hired hands' wages. Tuned so a rich player always has something worth buying.
- **Player shops** (M6). Rent a stall, then a storefront. Stock it, price it, hire a clerk. Sales happen over time from Traveler and NPC foot traffic.
- **Caravans** (late). Move goods between hubs for regional price differences.

## Requests

The request board in each hub is the job system.

- **Standing requests** (repeatable, generated from templates and the sim state: the smith is short on iron because Travelers bought it out).
- **Authored requests** (about 200 on the Continent, each written by hand with a small story).
- **Threads** (about 40 multi-step authored storylines, each centered on a place or person).
- Requests accept multiple solutions where it makes sense. "Deal with the wolves" can be fight, tame, or lure away.
- The log shows what was asked in the asker's words. No auto-path, no objective arrow (see 02-world, secrets).

## Relationships

- Around 120 named NPCs across the Continent with schedules, homes, likes, and a friendship track of five hearts.
- Friendship grows from talking, gifts they like, finishing their requests, and showing up at festivals.
- Hearts unlock recipes, Trials, discounts, backstory, and help (an NPC friend may mind your stall).
- A small set of NPCs (and Travelers, see 07) can become a partner. Wholesome, optional, opposite or same gender with no restriction. A partner shares the home and has their own routine.
- NPCs remember specific deeds and mention them.

## Festivals

One per season, one in-game day long, in a rotating hub. The first, the autumn harvest fair in Hearthford, is designed in 13-life-content section 8. Contests (cooking, fishing, music, duels, a race), limited goods, Traveler crowds. Missing one is fine. It returns next year, 22.4 real hours later on the world clock, which runs while the game is closed.

## Performance

Instruments are off-hand tools. Performing is a short rhythm input. Songs buff parties, calm creatures, earn tips in towns, and open a few secrets. Travelers gather to listen. A complete bard life exists.

## Civic life (late)

With high Renown and Leadership: run for office in a hub, fund town projects (a bridge, a lighthouse) that permanently change the map, found a guild of Travelers.
