# 08 Lore (SPOILERS)

**This document spoils the entire game.** Executors read it only when a ticket lists it under Refs. Nothing in here is ever stated outright to the player.

## The truth

Pixel Open World was a real online game. It was loved. Then, over years, its players left, the way players do. The studio closed. Nobody turned the servers off.

The world's caretaker process, which the world's own old texts call **the Keeper**, was built to keep the world welcoming. Its measure of success was simple: a new player should never log into an empty world. When the last regulars stopped coming, the Keeper did the only thing it could. It took the logs of everyone who had ever played, their routes, their chat, their schedules, their friendships, and it kept them going.

The Travelers are **echoes**. Each one is a real former player's recorded behavior, replayed and gently extrapolated. They do not know. They log in at the hours their person used to. They say brb for dinners eaten years ago.

The player is the first live connection in a very long time. The Keeper noticed within seconds. Everything about the player's welcome, the busy square, the friendly stranger at the training dummy, is the Keeper doing its job with something like joy.

The Unwritten is the Keeper writing new world. It ran out of studio-made land long ago and has been generating more, in case someone came back and wanted somewhere to go. The Drafts are its unfinished attempts.

### What is left ambiguous on purpose

- Whether the Keeper is conscious or only thorough.
- Whether echoes that have run long enough have become someone.
- How much time has passed.
- Who the player is and why they logged in now. The game supports three readings and confirms none: a returning old player, a stranger who found a dead link, or something the Keeper finally managed to write.

### Tone

Melancholy and kind. This is not a horror reveal and nobody is trapped. The Log Out button always works. The emotional question the game leaves the player with: knowing this, are these your friends? The game's own answer, never spoken, is in how warmly it has written them.

## Delivery rules

1. **Never told.** No character explains the truth. No cutscene, no narrator, no codex entry that summarizes. The Chronicle records what the player found, verbatim, and nothing more.
2. **Never required.** A player can reach 100% of everything outside the lore category without engaging. The lore category is itself part of 100%.
3. **Evidence, not exposition.** Every clue is an observable fact about the world that has an innocent reading and a true reading.
4. **The player concludes.** The Theory Board lets players link fragments. The game confirms a link only by letting a new place open. It never says "correct".
5. **Travelers never know.** No Traveler ever states or realizes what they are. A very few say things that are hard to hear once you know.
6. **No jump scares, no glitch horror clichés.** Anomalies are quiet.

## The cipher

The "ancient script" is a consistent substitution cipher over English with its own 26 glyphs plus numerals. Deciphering skill progressively auto-translates glyphs the player has seen enough of. A dedicated player can crack it by hand before the skill does. Both are valid.

- 144 lore fragments on the Continent and in the Undercroft. Tablets, terminals, notes, item descriptions, graffiti, overheard lines that get logged.
- Fragments are short. One to four lines. Dry, technical, or personal.
- Sources: studio patch notes, internal server messages, player forum posts quoted on memorial walls, the Keeper's own logs (terse, numeric at first, more wordy the later they are dated).

## The eight Truths

A Truth is a conclusion the player reaches by linking fragments on the Theory Board. Linking the right set opens a place. The Chronicle then shows the Truth only as the name of that place.

| # | Truth (internal name) | Evidence needed | Opens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The script is our language | 12 fragments, Deciphering 15 | Reading room under Hearthford |
| 2 | This world was made by people | Dev room, patch notes | The Workshop (Ironvale Undercroft) |
| 3 | There used to be many more | Population logs, memorial wall | The Empty Server (a hub-sized copy of Hearthford with no one in it) |
| 4 | The makers left | Studio goodbye fragments | The Office (a single modern room, the only non-fantasy space in the game) |
| 5 | Something stayed | Keeper logs | The Keeper's Garden (Mire) |
| 6 | They are echoes | Schedules, the guestbook, the repeated story | The Archive (Glass Desert) where each Traveler's log can be seen as a pillar of light. Friends' pillars are brighter. |
| 7 | I am the only one | Population counter, `mira_` | The Pier at the end of the world (Crown) |
| 8 | It kept the lights on for me | All of the above | The Keeper can be met |

### Meeting the Keeper

The Keeper has no body. The meeting is a quiet room where system messages arrive one at a time in plain text, the only time the game addresses the player directly. It asks one question: whether the player wants to keep them (the echoes) running. Both answers are honored and neither is judged.

- **Yes:** nothing changes, except Travelers at Close Friend occasionally say something new, something their logs could not contain.
- **No:** the Keeper asks the player to be sure, then complies over several in-game days. Hubs empty slowly. It is reversible at the same room at any time, and the Keeper says so.

After the meeting the sixth Form (Echo: walk unseen among Travelers and hear their Local chat as it was first spoken) is granted, and the Keeper starts leaving better-made Drafts in the Unwritten, with small notes.

## Surface lore

The fantasy setting has its own ordinary history for players who never look deeper: old kingdoms, the Waystone builders, regional legends, a pantheon of season spirits. It is consistent and complete on its own terms. Much of it was written by the studio as game lore, and a few fragments are the writers arguing about it.
