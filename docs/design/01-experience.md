# 01 Experience

## Player fantasy

"I have a second life in this world, and it is mine." The player is a person who logs in. The avatar is who they are here. The world has other people in it who know them, expect them, and get on with things when they are away.

Three promises the game makes and must keep:

1. **Whatever I spend time on, I become.** There is no wrong way to live here.
2. **The world noticed.** Travelers greet a known chef differently from a known duelist. Shops stock what you sell them. Rankings move.
3. **There is always something I have not found.** Even at 100%.

## Core loops

**Moment (seconds).** Move, strike, dodge, gather, tap a prompt. Every action feeds a skill.

**Session (3 to 60 minutes).** Log in. Collect what your Routines produced. See what changed (chat, market, a friend online, weather, a world event timer). Pick a thing: a request from the board, a dungeon, a recipe you lack an ingredient for, an unexplored corner. Come back, craft, sell, upgrade. Log out somewhere that matters (bed at home gives a rested bonus).

**Arc (hours).** Push into a harder region. Raise a skill to its next mastery rank and pass the Trial. Earn a Path. Build a home, then a business. Automate what you have mastered so your attention moves to the next thing.

**Long arc (tens of hours).** Notice things that do not add up. Collect fragments. Form a theory. Decide what you believe.

## No main quest

There is no quest log entry that says "save the world". Structure comes from four sources that pull without pushing:

- **The map.** Regions have fixed tiers. Harder land is visibly out there.
- **The request boards.** Every town posts jobs. This is the dependable "what do I do next".
- **Travelers.** They invite, challenge, ask for help, and talk about places you have not been.
- **Curiosity.** Sealed doors, unreadable tablets, a Traveler who never logs out.

A soft spine exists for players who want one: each region has a Waystone network to restore and a regional boss guarding the pass to the next tier. The game never calls this the main quest.

## The first hour

Target experience, minute by minute. Ticket POW-M1-10 and POW-M12-05 implement and polish it.

| Minute   | Beat                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0        | Title screen is a login screen. One button: Log In.                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| 0 to 2   | Avatar setup, presented as the game's own setup screen. Name, body, face, hair, colors, an optional origin (flavor only). No class, no stats.                                                                                                                    |
| 2        | You wake outside Hearthford at one of eight wake points (a hayfield, a ditch by the east road, under the old bridge, a hunter's lean-to, and so on), at whatever time of day it is. One bread, a wooden dagger, ragged clothes. World chat is already scrolling. |
| 2 to 5   | The town is in sight: mill sails, chimney smoke, the Waystone's light at night. No arrow points at it. Controls are learned from one-line contextual hints that appear the first time an action is possible and never again.                                     |
| 5 to 10  | The walk in is the first lesson. A berry bush awakens Foraging. A Mossling patch warns before it bites. Something on the way is worth picking up.                                                                                                                |
| 10 to 15 | Through the gate. The square has the Waystone, the request board and the notice wall. Workshops, the inn and the market are up the streets. Touching the Waystone or sleeping at the inn moves your respawn there.                                               |
| 15 to 30 | Starter requests teach gathering, combat and the cook pot by use. First skills awaken and rank up. A teacher in town offers a first technique. First crafted gear replaces the rags.                                                                             |
| 30 to 60 | First cave, the Rootway, which is hard on rags and a dagger and fair with what the first half hour gave you. First miniboss. First odd thing: a tablet you cannot read, and a line in chat that does not fit.                                                    |

Rule: in the first hour the player is never stopped by a modal they did not open.

Rule: the first hour starts poor on purpose (D19). The rags, the dagger and the single bread are the baseline every upgrade is measured against. Nothing in the first hour needs more than the town can give a player who walks in with nothing.

## Session shapes the design must serve

| Session                       | Must be able to                                                                 |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 3 minutes, one hand, standing | Collect Routine output, replant, sell, answer a friend, queue the next Routine. |
| 20 minutes                    | Finish one request or one mini-dungeon and bank the progress.                   |
| 2 hours                       | Clear a dungeon, reach a new region, or reorganize a homestead.                 |

Autosave is continuous. Quitting mid-anything loses nothing but position inside a dungeon room.

## Tone

Warm, a little funny, never cynical. The surface is a place you want to live. The hidden layer is melancholy, not horror. No gore, no cruelty for laughs. Travelers are written like people you might meet in a real online game on a good day.
